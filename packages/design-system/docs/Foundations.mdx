# Foundations

Lar's tokens live in `src/foundations/theme.css`, in two layers:

1. **Primitives**: fixed `--color-palette-*` steps: two pure-grey ramps (`grey` for light, `neutral` for dark), the
   white and black alpha ladder, navy, emerald, amber and red, including intermediate and alpha steps. ninna-ui's
   `base-*`, `primary` and status names are compatibility aliases selected per theme. Every semantic colour declaration
   references a fixed primitive directly; there are no exceptions.
2. **Semantic tokens**: what a colour is _for_ (a surface, text, an edge, a status's fill or text), built on the
   primitives. Components use these. Their values sit in one table, `src/foundations/tokens.ts`, which
   `tokens.test.ts` checks theme.css against and holds to WCAG AA: 4.5:1 for every text pair, 3:1 for control edges
   and the focus ring, in both themes.

The palette applies under `html[data-theme='minimal']`; `.light` or `.dark` on `<html>` picks a scheme, and with
neither it follows the system. Components read tokens through Tailwind utilities (`bg-surface-raised`,
`text-fg-muted`, `rounded-md`, `text-ui`, `@pane:`…); apps never use them at all.

### The vocabulary rule

Components speak the semantic layer only, and `foundations/vocabulary.test.ts` fails on anything else outside
`foundations/`:

- **No primitive.** `text-base-700`, `bg-base-200`, `border-base-300`, `var(--color-base-600)`: each is a role
  (`text-fg-muted`, `bg-fill-muted`, `border-line`, `var(--color-line-strong)`).
- **No `dark:` colour override.** Every semantic token already has its dark value; a component that needs a different
  colour in dark needs a token (or a value of an existing one) instead, which is then contrast-checked.
- **No `color-mix()` in a component.** A mix a component needs is a token (a hover fill, a tint); a translucent
  version of a role is Tailwind's opacity modifier (`bg-surface-page/85`, `border-warning/45`). An app's UI-layer sheets
  may mix in CSS, where their roles are derived.
- **An app's roles colour glyphs, dots and bars, never text**: text is always in a text token.

A genuine exception goes in that test's allow-list, with its reason; there are none today.

### Layering

`foundations`, `typography`, `actions`, `forms`, `display`, `feedback`, `navigation`, `overlays`, `collections` and
`layout` are all **generic**: they carry no product nouns and read only foundation tokens (`tokens.test.ts` fails on
any other custom property). Each app's **UI layer** (its `src/ui`) holds that app's own compositions: it composes the
generic layers and may add roles and sheets of its own, derived from the semantic tokens (a board's status roles, a
marketing page's device metal and glow, a media app's provider brand colours), which load from the app's CSS entry
after `styles.css`. Storybook loads `styles.css` alone, so a generic component that leans on an app's token shows the
gap here. A composition moves into the generic layers once a second product needs it.

| Import                                | Holds                                                             | Used by           |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| `@lar-works/design-system`            | the generic components, patterns and theme helpers                | every app         |
| `@lar-works/design-system/primitives` | the styleable `…Base` building blocks and the hooks they share    | an app's `src/ui` |
| `@lar-works/design-system/icons`      | every icon and the shared icon glossary (`conceptIcons`)          | every app         |
| `@lar-works/design-system/styles.css` | Tailwind, the tokens, base styles and every component's utilities | every app         |

over the `#fafafa` page in light (the light card, panel, control and field are white), so the same panel, card or
field reads on the page, on glass and on artwork. Bordered on black for anything that groups; filled for things. Dark
keeps T3 Code's balance: almost all black, with one dark step (`#0e0e0e`) for what floats; nothing at rest sits brighter
than white 6% but hover and selected fills and controls' edges.

**A deliberate deviation from WCAG 1.4.11.** A field's edge (`line-field`), a checkbox's or radio's edge
(`line-choice`) and a switch's off track (`fill-switch-off`) are not held to 3:1. The field's fill, its label and
placeholder (and a switch's white thumb) tell the control apart; what is held to 3:1 is its focus edge and ring and its
checked state (the primary fill).

**Same value, different role.** A few roles share a value in one theme or both, on purpose: each names what it is
for, so it can move on its own. `fg-lead` is `fg-muted` in light and a step lighter in dark, where long runs of
mid-grey tire the eye. `line-strong-hover` shares `fg-muted`'s step in light. Use the role you mean, never
the one whose value happens to match.

### Glass

Three tiers and a backdrop are the only translucency (`glassTiers` in tokens.ts; each palette's `--lar-glass-*`).
Every tier is a tint at an alpha under `saturate(1.8)` and a blur that deepens with the tier; without backdrop-filter
it falls back to its opaque tint.

| Tier                                                             | Utility                | Dark                                                     | Light            | Blur         | Edge and light                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Bar: the title bar, a site header                                | `glass-bar` (GlassBar) | `#000` at 60%                                            | `#fafafa` at 66% | 20px         | a hairline (white 8% / black 8%) once scrolled                           |
| Menu: menus, popovers, selects, tooltips, toasts                 | `glass-menu`           | `#141414` at 72% (≈ `#0e0e0e` over black)                | `#fff` at 82%    | 28px         | rim white 10%, inset top white 4%                                        |
| Dialog: dialogs, drawers, the command palette (its reading zone) | `glass-dialog`         | `#000` at 93%, no veil                                   | `#fff` at 94%    | 32px         | rim white 11%, inset top white 4%                                        |
| A Modal's glass zone: its sidebar                                | (Modal marks it)       | `#171717` at 70% (≈ `#111` over black), divider white 8% | `#f5f5f5` at 80% | the dialog's | the footer: white 3% / black 3% over the reading tint, divider white 10% |
| Backdrop under a dialog                                          | `dialog-backdrop`      | black 38%                                                | black 14%        | 4px          |                                                                          |

In dark the glass sits at T3 Code's balance, near black, so it reads through its blur and rim, not its brightness.
A Modal is two zones of one glass: the reading zone (header and content) is near-opaque ink, where only strong colour
bleeds through, and the blur shows in the glass zone, its sidebar (and faintly its footer), where you navigate and act.
The zones differ only by their fill, as a nested backdrop-filter can't see through the box's; the box paints both. The
values were set live against T3 Code's settings and confirm dialogs over the factory board and the server dashboard.
Inside a menu or a dialog, muted text rises a step (`#a3a3a3` / `#575757`), a panel becomes a faint well (white 3%,
edge white 8.5% / black 3%, edge black 8%) and `surface-raised` turns translucent, so nothing opaque sits on the glass;
inside a dialog a control is white 4% on a white 11% edge and the current place a subtler navy (12%). A dialog's header
inherits the reading zone and its footer is a faint light on it, set off by a divider, never a slab. `surface-glass`
is the bar's tint, for a panel over a backdrop or artwork.

## Status and progress

### One tone map

A status light and a progress bar take their colour from what the state **means**, the same in every app; the status
word beside the light stays in neutral text (the status-word rule in the Introduction), since the light already carries
the colour. Components take a `state` (`StatusState`); pick the state, never the colour. A `tone` remains for the rare case no
state names (a data colour, the accent for "in development").

| `state`   | Colour | Means                                                        | Examples                                            |
| --------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| `ready`   | green  | connected, ready, up to date, done                           | Lidarr connected, catalog up to date, merged        |
| `active`  | blue   | running, working, live                                       | scanning, 5 active tasks, building                  |
| `waiting` | amber  | waiting on something, needs attention, degraded, backing off | Remote access on, waiting; needs you; provider busy |
| `failed`  | red    | failed, broken, unreachable                                  | scan failed, folder not found                       |
| `off`     | grey   | off, idle, queued, not checked yet, unknown (no glow)        | turned off, not scanned yet                         |

`StatusIndicator`, `StatusRow`, `ToolbarStatusItem`, `PopoverHeader`, `StatusPopoverContent` and `ProgressStatusRow`
all take it. A state that is **more than one thing** ("5 active · 3 failed") is a `StatusSummary` (or an array of parts
for `PopoverHeader status` / `StatusPopoverContent status`): each part keeps its own light and colour, so a failure
never hides in the main state's blue. Leave out a part with nothing to count.

### One progress grammar

Every running piece of work (a scan, a job, an enrichment pass, a calibration) is a `ProgressStatusRow`, in a list, a
card's footer, a popover or a dialog alike:

1. **Identity first.** The light, then what or where (`label`: the catalog, "Video calibration"), never cut; it wraps.
   Inside the object's own card or dialog, which already names it, the label is the work ("Comparing with Lidarr").
2. **Second line, muted:** the phase (`operation`), the status word in neutral text (`statusLabel`: Queued, Waiting,
   Done), then the numbers in one format: **count · percent · ETA** ("1,204 of 3,890 files · 31% · 12 min left"). The
   caller words the count and the ETA; the row computes the percent. One extra fact may follow (`detail`); a full
   counter list belongs in the details the row opens (`onDetails`), never in the row.
3. **The bar**, in the status's colour: while running, determinate when the amount is known (`progress`), sweeping
   when it isn't; while paused or backing off, held at its place in amber. Queued, failed and done draw none.
4. **Why** it is held or failed (`reason`), in neutral text: "MusicBrainz is busy; trying again at 8:21 PM",
   "Folder not found: /mnt/media/tv".
5. **Actions** (Cancel, Retry, Review) at the end, centred on the text; the bar keeps to the text's column.

| `status`  | State     | Bar               | Words                             |
| --------- | --------- | ----------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `queued`  | `off`     | none              | "Queued", and what it waits for   |
| `running` | `active`  | fills, or sweeps  | count · percent · ETA             |
| `paused`  | `waiting` | held at its place | "Waiting"/"Paused" and the reason |
| `failed`  | `failed`  | none              | the reason, and Review or Retry   |
| `done`    | `ready`   | none              | "Done", and when it finished      |

Two densities: `compact` for card footers and popovers (12px label, 4px bar; an idle row keeps the bar's room so a
grid of cards holds its height), regular for pages and dialogs (14px label, 6px bar).

**What wraps and what stays on one line.** The label is the thing's identity and wraps, never cut. The meta line is
one line on purpose, so a list of rows keeps one rhythm: out of room it ends in an ellipsis while the percent stays
whole at its end, and the full line is the meta's hover title and, with the percent, the progress bar's accessible
value. The reason under a bar keeps to one line the same way, with its full text as its title. A card's summary
(`layout="line"`) puts the label in that one line too and ends it on the numbers, which stay whole with the percent:
the label and the words before them (the task, the status, the reason) give way first, under the same hover title.
The details it opens (`onDetails`) hold everything in full.

### Lights and legends

A **round** mark is a status light: only a state colours it. A **square** mark (`LegendSwatch`, drawn by `MeterLegend`)
is a series' key in a meter or chart: its colour names what the series is, never how it is doing. A row that names a
series and has a status (an encode device's playback job) takes the swatch as its indicator and says the status in
words, or adds a light coloured by its state.

A series takes its colour from its own set, never from a status hue: `series` 1, 2 and 3 (navy, teal, magenta; the
`series-1` to `series-3` tokens), in that order and never cycled, validated apart for every colour vision and 3:1 on a
card in both themes. Past three series, fold the rest into one or split the chart.

### One alignment rule

Wherever a status has lines under it (`StatusRow` with a `detail`, `ProgressStatusRow`, an attention row), the light
sits on the centre of the label's **first** line, never centred across the lines; a muted value reads with that line
and controls stay centred on the row. Cards side by side in a row (check-up summary cards, dashboard cards) top-align
their content, so their first lines line up whatever each card holds.

A row's leading icons come in one order: the **status mark first** (a `StatusIndicator` with a `glyph`, or its dot
standing in for one), **then the source icon** (where its ticket is saved), then the title. A glyph sits in a fixed
16px box (14px at `sm`), so a list's titles start at the same x whichever mark each row has; at work it turns
(`motion="spin"`, a spinner when it has no glyph) or breathes (`motion="pulse"`), on the motion tokens, paused out of
view and still for reduced motion. A dot is 8px with its 3px halo everywhere; the 6px `sm` dot is only for a 20px chip.
Work that moves through parts (stages, slices, parts, an activity) is one `SegmentedProgress` track. Rows in one status
list keep one rhythm: a row whose value is a control (`StatusRow trailing`) is as tall as a row whose value is a word.

## Layout rules for actions and options

- **Ghost actions sit flush.** In any action slot of a header or row (`SettingsSection action`, `SectionTitle actions`,
  `FormSection action`, `PopoverHeader action`, `SettingsRow` controls), a ghost Button (`quiet`, `destructive-quiet`)
  at the end lines its label up with the card's edge, like a bordered button's edge; the slot does it, so callers never
  nudge a button. A caption about a section's action belongs in the section's `description`, never under the button.
- **An action under prose is outlined.** Outside those slots nothing pulls a ghost's 12px inset back, so its label
  would start off the text above it. An action that follows a paragraph or a caption ("This album isn’t in
  MusicBrainz" under its explanation) is a `secondary` Button (`size="sm"` beside captions), whose edge lines up with
  the text; supporting prose above it keeps the reading measure (`Text measure="reading"`).
- **A footer option is a Checkbox.** `Modal.Footer leading` may hold a one-shot option for this submit
  ("Scan for changes now", "Create issue") as a `Checkbox`. A `Switch` there is a misuse: a switch sets something that
  stays, which is a row in the form.
- **Empty lists.** An empty settings or archive list is `EmptyState size="compact"` in the list's panel, with the add
  action when there is one ("No catalogs yet" · **Add catalog**).
- **Menus keep one inset.** A menu row with a control at its end (the account menu's Appearance choice) is
  `DropdownMenu.ControlRow`, which keeps the entries' 12px inset; never an app's `Inline` inside the menu. A menu's
  entries either all lead with an icon or none do: a lone icon (only Sign out) reads as another kind of entry.
- **No hard cuts.** Every region the design system scrolls (popovers, menus, modal and drawer bodies, image pickers,
  counter lists) fades where content runs past an edge; an app never adds its own scroller inside one.
- **Help opens beside what it explains.** `HelpTip` opens above its "?" (`side` for another side), so it never covers
  the description under the setting's name.
- **One splash.** `AppSplash` (and an app's own loading screen built on `SplashView`) shows one status line, which always ends in an
  ellipsis, over the calm indeterminate track while the app waits, in every app.
- **Every notice is an Alert, one recipe per tone.** The tone's quiet surface (`-surface`), its hairline (`-ring`) and
  its icon; the words are the primary text colour, title semibold, message regular, at either size. ConfirmDialog's
  reassurance note is an `info` Alert; no app or composition draws a notice box of its own.

## Radii

One radius per kind of surface, the same in every app: `md` 6px every control, field and the title bar's search;
`lg` 8px menus, popovers, toasts and artwork; `xl` 12px every card, panel and group (InsetPanel, RowList, a settings
group, ResultList's panel, anything with `surface-card` or `surface-panel`); `2xl` 16px dialogs, drawers and the
command palette; `3xl` 20px a marketing page's cards and bands. `xs` 4px is for the smallest things (a code chip, a
swatch, a text button's focus box) and `sm` 5px for a menu row, a tooltip or a chip. A surface's children never take
their own radius at its edge (a panel's footer is clipped by the panel). These are the only radii, with `full` and
`none`: no bare `rounded`, no Tailwind step outside these (`4xl`), no arbitrary `rounded-[…]` and no radius in rem in a
stylesheet. The marketing devices' frames, drawn to scale, are the one exception.

## Links

One link look, `typography/LinkRecipe.ts`, drawn by `Link`, `InlineLink`, RichText's and the Markdown editor's links
and every link a composition draws itself (a linked fact, a reference, "Part of #12"): an underline at 40% of the
link's colour that fills in on hover. Its tone is `accent` (`primary-fg`, the default: a link that stands on its own),
`inherit` (the colour of the text around it, inside a note or an alert) or `muted` (a navigation trail, such as
Breadcrumbs, whose underline shows on hover).

## Spacing and density

Every length is on Tailwind's 4px spacing scale (`--spacing: 0.25rem`): `p-3` is 12px, `max-w-160` 640px, `px-2.75`
11px. A page's content is at most `max-w-page` (1200px) wide.

**Density by surface, never by app.** The size of a control follows the surface it sits on, the same in every app;
no app picks a denser or looser size for itself:

| Size | Height | Where                                                                              |
| ---- | ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `sm` | 28px   | the title bar, toolbars, row and card actions, section-header actions              |
| `md` | 32px   | forms, settings rows, every dialog footer, empty states, auth (`Button`'s default) |
| `lg` | 44px   | marketing pages only (`ButtonLink size="lg"`)                                      |

Both app sizes take the 13px/500 label (`text-ui`). A coarse pointer reaches any button at 44px through an invisible
hit area, without growing what it draws. Icon buttons, the title bar's and a dialog's close alike, are 28px with a
16px glyph; every pressable control dips to 97% while pressed. Rows are 40px single-line, 60px two-line and 28px in a
menu. Arbitrary lengths (`w-[340px]`, `gap-[3px]`) fail `scales.test.ts`; the few pixel-exact glyphs it
allows (a 1.5px ring, a 7px monogram) are listed there with their reasons.

App shells use one 16px gutter, a spacing token, `px-gutter-app` (`--spacing-gutter-app`, 16px: `ContentContainer`,
`TitleBar`), for the bar and the content alike, in every app, a desktop window's board included. Shell layout owns this
spacing; neither an app nor a page adds a gutter of its own.

**The title bar is one bar in every app** (`TitleBar`, 52px; the site's `SiteHeader` 56px, both on `glass-bar`). On
the left, the lockup (`product` and `home`: the mark, "ar" and the product's word at 20/500 muted, always a link home,
the word hidden under 640px) and where you are (`TitleBarTrail`, at most 280px). At the end, one group in a fixed
order, each a slot of its own: `search` · `status` · `actions` (the app's) · `settings` · `identity`, every control 28px
on a 6px radius, 8px apart, each icon-only one named by a glass tooltip (never a native `title`). The bar never wraps:
search folds to its icon by the bar's width, and `status` folds into a "…" popover under 40rem. A web app's shell
lets the document scroll; a desktop window's (`AppShell scroll="contained"`) scrolls its own main area under the bar,
which drags the window and starts past the traffic lights (`trafficLights`). Popovers come in three widths: 256px a
menu (`Popover.Content width="sm"`), 320px a status (`StatusPopoverContent`'s default) and 384px a list
(`StatusPopoverContent width="wide"`, as a `ToolbarCounter`'s).

## Elevation

Shadows by role, deeper in the dark theme. They are plain variables, used as `shadow-(--shadow-raised)`;
`glass-menu` and `glass-dialog` already carry theirs. A component never mixes a shadow of its own: `scales.test.ts`
fails on a `shadow-[…]`, `drop-shadow-[…]` or `inset-shadow-[…]` that names a colour. Besides the four surfaces,
a solid fill's lit top edge is `inset-shadow-(--shadow-highlight)` (pressed: `--shadow-pressed`), and a logo over
artwork lifts off it with `drop-shadow-(--shadow-logo)`. An app's UI layer may add its own in its sheets, such as a
marketing page's device shadows.

**Card, panel, raised.** A card resting on the page takes `surface-card` (its faint fill, `line-card` edge and
`--shadow-card`: in light a hairline drop so a white card reads off the `#fafafa` page; in dark none, as the fill and
edge carry it on black). A panel, a group of rows, takes `surface-panel` and no shadow. A control or a field resting
on the page (an outlined button, a text field, a chip) takes the flatter `--shadow-raised`, so a control never reads as
a card and a card full of controls keeps one level. Popups and dialogs sit above both, their shadows carried by
`glass-menu` and `glass-dialog`.

## Destructive actions

The quiet-destructive rule (Introduction): a trigger that opens a destructive confirmation is named in the danger text
colour at rest, in a quiet weight, and gains the danger surface on hover or keyboard focus: `Button
variant="destructive-quiet"`, `IconButton tone="danger"` and `DropdownMenu.Item destructive`. It is never neutral grey
text. Only the confirming action in `ConfirmDialog` uses a solid danger fill (`Button variant="destructive"`). This
applies equally to deleting a library, deleting logs and abandoning work. A state word is never in the danger text colour
(the status-word rule); a problem's explanation stays in neutral text. The one sentence in the danger text colour is a
field's error under its invalid control (`Field errorText`), which pairs with the control's danger edge.

## Writing

The **Writing / Content** foundations page defines the shared glossary: sentence case, no trailing full stops in
headings or labels, UK English with **catalog** as the deliberate product term, chart units in axes and values, and
one arrow after standalone navigation instructions (`Link arrow`). Settings headings and actions remain in place
while their row values load; use row-shaped skeletons rather than a single block.
